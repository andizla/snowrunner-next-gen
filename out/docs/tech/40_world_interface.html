Scenery detail and grass reach

Cards: Scenery detail (rows: the near switches, hide, shadows), Grass reach.

Levels of detail

Each mesh carries simpler versions of itself and the distances at which the game switches to them. Many meshes swap 10 to 15 m from the camera. With GTAO and sharper shadows, that swap shows as a flip in shading at a fixed distance, rock outcrops most of all. The meshes live in shared.pak (2 GB) as "[meshes]\models_<name>" and "[meshes]\plants_<name>": each a 32-bit size, an XML header, then binary data. The header holds the switches:

<MeshLod Distances="10, 25, 45" ... />

A mesh whose first switch is nearer than 40 m gets its switches raised to at least 40, 70, 110 and 160 m; a value already further stays. The card's set is either "nature" (rocks, trees and bushes by name; stumps, twigs, crops, ruins, walls and signs left out) or every mesh with switches. Two extra rows take out the distance beyond which a mesh is no longer drawn (HidingDistance), and the tag that ends a small thing's shadow or keeps it from casting one (MeshShadow), for plants or for everything.

The XML must keep its byte length, so the binary data after it and its size prefix stay byte for byte: the whitespace between tags gives or takes the difference.

Re-compressing only what changed

shared.pak is a zip; many of its entries are deflate-compressed, and a mesh's XML sits at the start of its stream. A deflate stream is a series of blocks, each with its own Huffman tables. Re-compressing whole meshes would take long; the tool instead re-encodes only the blocks that hold the XML header and appends the rest of the original compressed bit stream as it is, shifted to its new bit position (a block need not start on a byte boundary). The result is inflated again and checked before the pak is written. Unchanged entries are copied raw, stored ones stay stored.

Grass reach

Grass meshes carry no switches; the game fades grass by distance per grass type, in initial.pak:

<GrassBrand FadeDistances="7, 17, 30">   density steps, then the end of the grass

Most grass is gone at 30 m. The card multiplies the distances by its factor, with one limit: the game draws grass only to about 64 m from the camera, and a step set beyond that no longer fades, so the grass at the edge appears whole at once (found by Milliath). Step n of k therefore ends at most at

d'n = max(dn, min(f · dn, 60 − 5 (k − 1 − n)))

which gives 21, 51, 60 m at 3 and 14, 34, 60 m at 2 for the common "7, 17, 30"; a step the game itself sets farther (some brands end at 80 to 150 m) stays. Cost: more grass blades drawn, which on dense meadows is the card's real cost.

Map days

Card: Map days. Which region's time-of-day states and sky each map cycles through.

Where a map names its days

Each level's pak (level_us_06_01.pak) holds [scene]\data_for_ssl.bin, stored, which begins with its names, each a 16-bit length, the text and a zero: the level's name, its description, then the states of the night, the dawn, the three days (each a ; list of a state and its weather variants), the dusk, and the region's sky file. Four regions ship no states of their own and name another region's: Maine names Yukon's and Alaska's, British Columbia Michigan's, Austria Belozersk's and North Carolina's, Washington North Carolina's. The game takes the names as they stand: the file's own check value is not compared (tested in the game), and any state initial.pak knows can be named. A name it does not know stops the load.

The edit

Every region code in the head is replaced by another of the same length (us_04 → us_01), in the player's own file, in place. The zip's CRC of the entry follows in both of its places, the local header and the central directory. Nothing is kept for the way back: Restore writes the game's codes, which the tool's table holds, and the CRC comes out as it was. A level file another mod changed is left as it is. Apply and Restore check that the level files can be written only when they may write them (Map days chosen or in), so a read-only level file stops no run that leaves the maps alone.

The choices

Each level's snow and ice textures were counted from its load list (Yellowrock National Forest has two to four times the snow and ice of The Lowland, which is at Michigan's level), and each region's season taken from the wiki and checked against the game (Scandinavia, filed under spring, is late winter there, with snow). The map by map value then picks per level: The Lowland Michigan's days, Yellowrock Yukon's spring with its blizzards turned to flurries, Duncan Bay Wisconsin's summer with its heavy rain, North Peak North Carolina's, Unterfeld Tennessee's, Washington Michigan's autumn and its Mountains Zurdania's. Every target region has all ten state names each level uses.

Sets of a map's own

Maine's two maps get sets of their own, us_91 and us_92, numbers no region of the game uses. A set is made from a source region's states and sky as they stand in the build: the files under the new names, the state names inside the sky file (DayTimeOverride DayTime="day__1_us_04") renamed with them, and the weather changed by a table (Yellowrock: heavy_snow becomes light_snow at 0.4 of its intensity). The engine adds the 20 files to initial.pak, stored, at the end, with their directory records where their names belong; then it names every file in the pak's load list, without which the game does not know them (next chapter). A level file names a set only while initial.pak holds it: the tool puts the set in before writing the level files, and takes it out after none names it. If a game update replaces initial.pak while a level still names a set, the status reads "part" and the next Apply mends it. Restore keeps initial.pak as it is while a level file still names a set: with one of Maine's two files changed by another mod the level files are not put back, and the game's own initial.pak, which holds no such set, would stop the other map from loading; Restore says so instead.

The load list: how a pak tells the game what it holds

The engine part behind Map days' own sets, and behind any file added to a pak.

The first entry of every pak, pak.load_list, lists everything the pak loads and in what order: a start, stages ("TEMPLATES load", "CLASSES load", "TEXTURE load", "MESH load"), the assets between them, an end. A class file (a truck, a daytime state, a sky) that is in the pak but not in this list is unknown to the game: a level that names it crashes. That was tested in the game: twelve states added to initial.pak without list entries crashed the level; the same states with entries loaded.

The layout follows the reading in SnowRunnerTools by chase-000:

PartBytes
headi32 1, u8 1, i32 count, i32 3, u8 1
typescount bytes: 5 the start, 1 a stage, 2 an asset, 6 the end; then u8 1
waitsper entry: i32 n, u8 1, n × i32, the entries it waits for (counted from 0, ascending); then u8 1
stringsper entry: i32 s, i32 2, s + 2 bytes of 01, then s strings (u32 length and text)

An asset has three strings (its name, its loader, its pak: "<media>\classes\daytimes\x.xml", "cls_loader", "initial.pak") or four (a texture in a level pak: a JSON text naming its base). The rules the game's own lists keep: an asset waits for one entry (the stage before its run, or, for a texture's faces, its header); a stage after assets waits for exactly those nothing else waits for; a stage after a stage waits for that stage; the class files stand together in the byte order of their names. The tool reads all 73 of the game's lists and writes them back byte for byte. Adding a class file means: the new entry where its name belongs among the class files, waiting for the stage before them; every number naming a later entry raised by one; the stage after the class files waiting for the new one too.

Next Gen logo and the start without the intro

Card: Next Gen logo (row Start-up).

The logos

NEXT GEN appears under the game's logo on five textures in gfx.pak. Three are pictures of their own (the title screen, the first loading screen at 3840 × 2160, the main menu) and are replaced whole. Two are sheets of many sprites (the pause menu, 500 × 504, and the map screen, 512 × 508), where only the logo sprite should change, so that an interface mod's other sprites stay. The sheets are BC7: every 4 × 4 block of texels is 16 bytes of its own, independent of its neighbours. So the tool copies exactly the blocks inside the logo sprite's rectangle (multiples of 4 texels) from Next Gen's sheet into the sheet the pak holds, and every other block stays bit for bit. A sheet of another size or format is left as it is.

The start without the intro

The game's intro is not a video: one Flash (Scaleform) movie in gfx.pak's [gfx]\gfxbundle.gfxbundle runs it, at 35 frames a second. Frames 2 to 259 show the epilepsy and legal warning (7.4 s); a script on frame 259 stops the movie and tells the loading screen it is ready (OnSkippableReadyToStart); the loading screen then calls StartSkippables, which plays the publisher logos (about 11 s, skippable by a key); at their end OnAllAnimFinished hands over (OnLogoEnd) and the bootup loading screen shows: the Next Gen splash. The row edits the movie's ActionScript bytecode, in the movie and its Japanese twin:

Each edit keeps its size, so the bundle's header (which lists the movies' names and lengths, no offsets or checksums) stays valid; only the pak's zip CRC of the entry changes. Each edit is found by the whole method's bytes as the game ships them; a game version whose movie differs keeps its intro. Measured: the title screen came 41 s after start with the intro and 24 to 25 s without (warm disk cache). The loading behind the intro still has to finish; the saving is the part of the intro that outlasted it.
