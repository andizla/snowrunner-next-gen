Reflection cubes from photographs

Card: Reflection cubes. The painted sky cubes that paint and chrome mirror and the ambient light reads, made anew from photographs for every region and hour.

What a reflection cube is in SnowRunner

Each daytime state names one cube (EnvMap="env/day_al_01__d_ggxrefl.tga"; in boot.pak a .pct texture). It has six square faces of 256 × 256 texels in Direct3D's order +X, −X, +Y, −Y, +Z, −Z, compressed as BC7, with 8 mip levels down to 2 × 2, each a plain downsample of the one before. Materials sample it at mip √roughness × 8 + 0.5, so the mips stand for rougher and rougher reflections. Sky ambient and Bounce light read its last level as the sky's and the ground's colour. The game shares a few painted cubes between all regions. Next Gen makes 25 of the 26 the states name from 21 photographic panoramas (CC0, Poly Haven; the garage keeps the game's).

From a panorama to six faces

A panorama in equirectangular form maps every direction to a point of a 2:1 picture. For a direction d = (x, y, z), y up:

φ = atan2(x, z),   θ = asin(y),   u = ½ + φ / 2π,   v = ½ − θ / π

Each face texel is a direction (the texel's centre on the face of a cube around the origin, normalised); make_cube.js looks the panorama up at 3 × 3 points inside the texel and averages them, so the faces are not aliased. The source is Radiance HDR (RGBE: a shared exponent per texel), linear light.

Exposure, colour, and the game's brightness

A photograph has its own exposure. The cube is scaled so that its upper half (the sky) lands at the brightness of the stock cube's sky, an sRGB luminance (SKY_MEAN). Options set the sky straight overhead and the ground apart from the sky, and white-balance a dusk, dawn or night to the hue of the game's cube for that hour. Then the texels are encoded sRGB, as the game's are, and box-filtered down the mips: each texel of mip k + 1 is the mean of four of mip k.

BC7 encoding

BC7 stores each 4 × 4 block in 16 bytes. The tool uses mode 6: one subset, two RGBA end points of 7 bits per channel plus a shared low bit each, and a 4-bit index per texel that picks one of 16 points on the line between them. For a block of texels ci, the indices are chosen by projecting each texel on the line, then the end points A and B are refined by least squares for those indices:

minimise Σi | ci − ((1 − wi) A + wi B) |²,   wi = indexi / 15

which is a 2 × 2 linear system per channel. The shared low bit is the one that rounds the four channels best, except that an end point whose alpha is 0 or 255 keeps it exact: picked for the colour, the bit had made a clear sky's alpha 1 here and 0 there, in rings that followed the colour's gradient, and the night states' multipliers (× 3 on Michigan's back bands) drew them as outlines around the clouds (seen in the game). The file's 138 header bytes and its 6-byte tail are copied from the stock cube, so the game sees exactly the layout it ships with.

The winter cube rule

The game names cubes per state by family, and two of its choices stop holding once the snow families' cubes are real snow photographs. Kola and Yukon give their clear days 1 and 2 the Michigan summer cube and only day 3 the snow cube; with the rule, a clear day of a region with snow that names a summer cube takes the snow cube its region's other clear days use. And a few states of regions without snow name a snow cube (a weather variant of Wisconsin, North Carolina's dusk); they take the cube their region's other states of the same kind use. The engine applies this to initial.pak whenever boot.pak holds the new cubes.

Night sky, brighter stars, skies

Cards: Night sky, Brighter stars, Skies.

Night sky: a real star map

The game's stars are one texture (4096 × 1024) wrapped around the sky, with stars a texel wide. Next Gen's is NASA's Deep Star Maps 2020 at 8192 × 2048: point stars at their catalogued brightness and the Milky Way's glow. The two photo night skies of Scandinavia, Kola and Quebec (one picture each, an aurora photograph) get the same star points painted in.

The glow is faint: at the galaxy's edge its light is 1 to 3 in the alpha's 255 steps, each step half again or more, which the layer's multiplier (up to × 5) showed as flat islands with hard edges along the band (seen in the game on Washington's night sky). Since the shader weights the colour by the alpha, the faint glow is stored as both: the alpha rounded up and the grey carrying the rest, so its light steps by alpha / 255 of a step. The glow under that is found again smooth from the texture itself (the smallest alpha in 5 × 5, which leaves the stars out, then a 17 × 17 box where it is faint and 5 × 5 where it is bright); the stars and the bright glow are as they were.

The BC7 encoder then undid part of that (seen in the game, 2026-10-31): its shared low bit left the empty sky at alpha 1 with white colour, a little brighter than the glow's faint edge, so a dark rim ran along the Milky Way, and it weighed an alpha step like a step of grey, so inside the glow the light snapped to whole alpha steps in contours. The star map is encoded again (out\skies_build\encode_raw.js with an alpha weight of 256, mips that average the light): the empty sky is 0, and the faint glow's light is off by 0.003 on average where it was off by 0.9.

Brighter stars: why the level must rise

A sky layer's brightness is "g(r; g; b; a) × N": N scales the colour, the alpha is a / 255, and the sky shader outputs the colour weighted by the alpha. The night states draw the star layer at × 0.07 to × 0.5, levels set for the stock texture's wide stars. NASA's point stars put about a quarter of the light into each star's texel, so at the stock levels they come out at 1 to 2 nits (the brightest at 6 to 9) on a sky of 0.15 to 0.4 nits; the texture's alpha is already at its 8-bit ceiling for the bright ones. So the card multiplies N in the night overrides of the star layer by one factor (dusk and dawn keep their level where a region shows stars then, as they are bright skies).

The photo skies cannot take that factor: one picture holds the photograph and the stars, so N would brighten the photo too. Their build carries the stars' share in the alpha instead: 85 where a texel is all photo, up to 255 on star points. The layer's own alpha is raised three times (50 → 150): the photo stays as it was (85 × 150 = 255 × 50), the star points come out three times as bright.

Skies: domes and cloud bands from photographs

A SnowRunner sky is layers on a sphere: a sky dome (sphere_sky) and up to three cloud bands (clouds_rear, clouds_mid, clouds_near) that turn around the map, each tinted per daytime state. The card (on by default since 1.1.0) replaces 49 of those textures, every region's, with ones made from 25 photographs of real skies: domes by make_cube.js at the stock texture's size and mean colour with the photograph's sun taken out, and cloud bands. Two night layers that are black but for a patch of the Milky Way get the real one: a strip around the galactic centre sampled from NASA's Deep Star Maps 2020 through the galactic to equatorial rotation, laid along the stock patch's long axis (its moments give the centre, tilt and spread), faded by an ellipse of that spread, and its light mapped onto the stock patch's own distribution of brightness, quantile for quantile. The cloud bands are cut from panoramas by make_clouds.js: a band is a texture 8 (or 4) times as wide as high whose alpha is the clouds' shape and whose colour is their shading in grey. What is cloud and what is sky is told by colour, since a clear sky is blue and a cloud is not:

b = (B − R) / (B + R),   bsky = 85th percentile of b in the row,   cloudiness = bsky − b

The alpha is a soft step over the cloudiness, set so that the band's mean alpha matches the stock band's coverage; the band fades out at its upper and lower edge as the game's do; the colour is the texel's luminance over the brightest cloud's, so a state's tint works on it as on the game's. The alpha is softened over its neighbours, so a cloud's soft edge reaches out over texels that are sky in the photograph; their grey is the sky's, darker than the cloud, and showed at night as a dark rim round every cloud (seen in the game, 2026-10-23). Averaging the grey over the same neighbours, each weighted by its alpha squared, did not take it away: a thin edge is part sky in the photograph and came out darker than its cloud (219 inside, 186 at the edge, 173 just outside in a near band), and filtering between texels and every smaller mip mixes that in. Now every texel carries the grey of the clouds near it: each grey weighted by its alpha to the fourth (a solid cloud counts, a thin edge hardly), summed down a pyramid of 2 × 2 cells and pulled back up, so a cloud fades out by its alpha alone. The smaller mips average the colour by alpha too, so a clear texel's colour does not count where cloud is near. On the top level the clear texels beside a cloud were 5 to 19 levels of 255 below the soft edge; now they are at most 8 below it, and in some bands above it.

The zenith: every column of a dome meets at the top, and the game closes its dome above about 81 degrees with a fan of triangles, so whatever a photograph has up there is squeezed into wedges around a point with a ring around it (seen in the game looking straight up, and as streaks running to the zenith on the overcast skies). From 66 degrees up each row of a dome, and of a cloud band that reaches that high, is blended towards its own mean colour and alpha, smoothly, wholly at 86 degrees: the sky straight overhead is plain, as in the game's own textures. Yukon's night dome (sky_01a_us_04_night) keeps its first build for now: rebuilt with the options on record it does not match the shipped texture, so it is not rebuilt from a guess. The near cloud band most maps use (cloud_03_us_01) is cut with a wider soft edge (0.45), its alpha softened over 3 texels and patches under 400 texels left out: at night small hard-edged fragments showed as specks against the stars. Around the photograph's sun a band holds only the fill, with no cloud of its own; cut clear there it left a round hole with a hard edge in the band. That stretch now takes the band's clouds from the far side, half way round at the same height, faded in over the fill's edge.

Twenty textures were made again for 1.1.1 after a look at every sky texture against shots from the game (2026-10-26). Kola's clear-day domes (sky_01_ru_03_day, sky_02_ru_03_day), the first ones built (2026-10-12), had a flat grey patch where the sun was taken out, a green and pink ring around it and the photograph's hills drawn into the sky; they are now Rustig Koppie and Kloppenheim 05. The layer over Kola's day 2 (sky_03_ru_03_day) had a dark notch at the horizon where its sun was taken out; it is now Qwantani Dusk 1 at 8192 × 2048. The five domes the game draws at 2048 × 512, one level with an 82-byte header (cloud_us_02_0044, 0049, 0237, 0331 and cloud_us_19_0001), are 8192 × 2048, the photographs' own size: their header takes the new width and height at 0x10, the level's size at 0x40 and the data's at 0x4E, and the tail the file's size. Built by out\skies_build\domes_111.ps1. cloud_02_us_01 is four times its size too, and make_clouds.js --drop-top leaves out whole every cloud that reaches into the upper half of the top edge's fade: a cloud is its solid part (over 0.35 before the fade, so thin haze does not join it to the clouds below) with its soft rim. The game showed one more cut flat along a straight line in Kola's clear day (2026-10-27): every cumulus band was then measured for solid cloud 8 % below its top (cloud_01_us_01 12 % of its width, cloud_01_ru_02 13 %, cloud_01_us_02 48 %, cloud_02_us_02 31 %, cloud_03_us_02 26 %) and made again with --drop-top, cloud_01_ru_02 and cloud_02_us_02 at 4096 × 512. In cloud_01_us_02 nearly every Sunflowers cloud reached the top, so it is now ambientCG's Day Sky HDRI 012 B.

cloud_02_ru_02 and cloud_02_ru_03, Kola's and Taymyr's back band, had plain white clouds with no shading in 1024 × 128 texels, so a dusk tint lit them as flat cut-outs, and a cloud that ran into the band's upper edge ended in a straight line; both were the same picture. They are now two photographs of low cumulus from ambientCG (Day Sky HDRI 065 B and 040 B, CC0) in 4096 × 512, cut with a lower coverage than the game's (0.24 and 0.13) so that the photographs' thin haze stays out. ambientCG ships its panoramas as OpenEXR with PIZ compression; out\skies_build\exr2hdr.py (Python with the OpenEXR package) writes them as the Radiance .hdr make_cube.js reads. The game reads a texture's size from its header, which holds no name, so a band takes the header of another band of the size it wants. make_clouds.js takes --top-edge, the share of the height the upper edge fades over (0.16 by default, as the game's): at 0.4 to 0.45 a cloud that reaches the edge thins out instead. cloud_03_ru_02 (plain white blobs) is now Sunflowers in 4096 × 1024, cloud_03_us_01 Wasteland Clouds, and cloud_04_ru_03, the grey layer of Kola's and Amur's day 1a and six of Zurdania's states, Aristea Wreck: it was Kloppenheim 07, the same smeared night photograph as Kola's overcast dome below. Each photograph was picked so that no daytime state shows it twice. Built by out\skies_build\bands_111.ps1 and build.ps1.

The near bands of Michigan's overcast days (cloud_03_ru_02 on day 3a, cloud_03_us_01 on 1a and 2a) are tinted at × 0.3, and the cut-out cumulus with their shaded undersides in the colour came out as opaque dark blue shapes with hard edges, stretched tall straight overhead (seen in the game on Black River). The game's own near band is plain white, and its alpha is the clouds' light: a lit top is opaque, a shaded underside thin, so a dark tint gives a soft grey veil. make_clouds.js --lum builds a band that way: the alpha is the soft step over the cloudiness times the texel's brightness over the band's brightest clouds, and the colour is white. cloud_03_ru_02 is now Kloofendal Overcast, a soft cloud deck, and cloud_03_us_01 keeps Wasteland Clouds, both with --lum. Every band's faintest alpha, under 0.04, also goes to 0 (--floor). The fifteen bands make_clouds.js built were made again with the encoder above (out\skies_build\bands_112.ps1); the others keep their texels.

The sun: the game draws its own, so the photograph's comes out, with its glare. The glare is measured as a gain over the sky on the far side, degree by degree out from the sun, and divided out. Each degree's gain comes from the ring of texels at that angle, ranked by brightness, from the ones a quarter of the way up: clouds near the sun are lit by it and stand above the sky, and the median of a ring half covered by them took the sky beside them too far down, a dark ring around the spot. The gains are eased from one degree to the next, since a step showed as a coloured ring. The disc itself, 2 to 6 degrees where the glare is over 30 times the sky, holds nothing usable and is filled over a 4 degree edge. The first fill carried each direction's colour straight in from the ring outside it, and a disc beside a cloud met at its centre as a pinwheel of cloud and sky with a ring around it (seen in the game on a clear day, 2026-10-23, where it looked like the zenith but stood at the sun's 48 degrees). Now it is a membrane over that ring: at the rim each direction keeps the colour that stands there, and towards the middle the differences fade, finer ones faster, to the ring's mean. On it goes the detail of a patch as big at the same elevation 60 to 300 degrees round the sky, as its brightness over that patch's own membrane (between half and twice), so a cloud deck carries on through the disc; of those patches, the one whose ring varies most like the disc's. Under a cloud sheet the glow is the sheet's own and stays, and the fill rises with it towards the middle, level from the disc's edge in: a level fill under the glow showed as a dark spot.

Where the fill still read as a patch in the game (a soft blurred disc where the sun had been, seen on 2026-10-23), and at the zenith, the spot is now repainted by an image model, Google's Gemini. engine\tools\sky_edit.js cuts flat views of a built dome: one centred on the sun's old place, 50 degrees wide with a circle of 10 degrees, and one straight up, 110 degrees wide with a circle of 35. Each goes to the model with the circle drawn in red and a prompt to repaint only inside it and take the circle out. Its apply puts the answers back. Each picture's colour is matched to the dome's own just outside the circle (the model shifts a whole picture a little: Zurdania's three answers were off by 1 to 2 levels of 255 there), taken wholly up to half a degree past the circle and faded out by 4, the zenith's view first, so the sun's edit has the last word where they meet. Then the cap: from 64 degrees up blended towards one colour, wholly at 80, the median of the sky between 76 and 80 degrees (their mean, with a cloud reaching the ring, left a lighter disc, and a blend over 7 degrees still showed its edge). Every level is encoded again from the edited one, and a before and after of each view is written beside it. The first two: Zurdania's clear day (cloud_04_ru_02_day_01, the sun at 48 degrees and the zenith) and its overcast (cloud_04_ru_02_day_02, the zenith), in the game since 2026-10-23. A dome built again from its panorama loses its edits until apply runs on it again; the views, the answers and their records are kept in out\gemini.

Three overcast domes were made again after they read as plain in the game (2026-10-24), where the game's own are heavy, stormy skies: Kola's day__2a, which its dawn uses too (sky_02a_ru_03_day), had come out as a fair sky of small clouds with a round hole where the photograph's sun had been taken out; Kola's day__3a (sky_03a_ru_03_day) as thin streaks running to one point near the horizon; Zurdania's overcast (cloud_04_ru_02_day_02) as an even grey. They are now made from Overcast Soil (both of Kola's, day__2a's turned 180 degrees so the two days do not show the same clouds) and Mud Road. Kloppenheim 07, which day__2a had first, is a night photograph: its clouds moved during the long exposure and showed in the game as smeared streaks (Kola's dawn, 2026-10-26). Overcast photographs are flat (all eight of Poly Haven's pure overcast skies are marked low contrast), so make_cube.js sky takes --contrast k, which spreads each texel's light around the sky half's geometric mean luminance, L' = G (L / G)k, the colour kept, before the mean colour is matched: 1.4, 1.4 and 1.6 here. No sun is taken out of them: their glow is soft, as in the game's own overcast textures. Built by out\skies_build\overcast.ps1.

The aurora: the two layers that hold it (cloud_01_ru_03 and cloud_01_us_11, the second the first's mirror image to within about 6 levels of 255) had it blue and violet, green only along its lower edge and in a curl. A real aurora is mostly green, the light of oxygen at 557.7 nm, often with red above it where the oxygen is higher and thinner (630 nm). It was cut out as a square on black, 2080 texels wide, and Gemini repainted it in those colours with its shape and curtains kept. The answer goes back scaled to the square, its black floor (3 of 255, JPEG noise that would veil the sky) taken off, its colour strengthened 1.6 times around each texel's own grey and its total light set to the stock aurora's (a gain of 0.78). The second texture is the first mirrored, and both keep the stock alpha. The answer, the crop and the scripts are kept in out\gemini\aurora.

Each texture goes in with its .pct_header, which the game keeps beside every .pct: the .pct's header with its last record (type 0xff, the length of the data after it) turned into type 1 with the header's own length, as all 349 pairs in the stock boot.pak have it. Until 2026-10-23 the tools wrote the .pct's head as it stands, and the domes made smaller to the stock size (4096 × 1024) kept an older header of 8192 × 2048; now every pair agrees.
