Smoke: glow, shape, softer edges

Cards: Smoke glow (row More kinds of smoke), Smoke shape and softer edges.

How the game lights a puff

Exhaust, dust, steam and smoke are sprites: flat textured squares that always face the camera. The game lights each with one sun value and one ambient value per vertex: either a baked top-down light map, or the sun's colour times a visibility factor. Nothing depends on where the sun is relative to the camera, and the sprite has no shape: a puff is a flat disc of light.

Glow: forward scattering

Smoke scatters sunlight mostly forward, so against a low sun it glows, often brighter than the sky behind it. The helper, spliced into the particle vertex shaders, multiplies the puff's sun light by

f = min(1 + 0.15 · max(4π pHG(μ; g = 0.7) − 1, 0), 2)

with μ the cosine between the view ray and the sun's light direction and pHG the Henyey-Greenstein phase function (see the fog chapter). 4π pHG is 1 for isotropic scattering, so f = 1 from 90 degrees away from the sun on, and rises towards the sun (looking straight at it, 4π pHG ≈ 18.9 and the cap of 2 applies). The factor rides on the sun term, so the pixel shader's shadow test, the light map's baked shade, the night fade and the fog still apply. Particles whose vertex shader gives them no sun light at all get the sun's colour times f − 1, the glow alone.

The particle vertex shaders are of a few families, and the patch has to find the sun term in each: a light map decoded from one register lane, the sun colour added, the sun colour times a visibility (plus the ambient). Of 205 vertex shaders, 162 take the glow by default. The row More kinds of smoke adds 8 whose light map decode sits in lanes x, z and w of its register (170 of 205). Of the other 35, 10 are normal-mapped puffs (lit in the pixel shader), 22 output no light, and 3 lack the scene constants.

Shape: a pseudo-normal from the alpha

The pixel-shader part (smoke_shade, in the 80 lit, blended puffs without a normal map of their own) gives each pixel the normal of a soft ball. Its direction on screen is the gradient of the puff's alpha (outward, where the alpha falls off); its tilt comes from the alpha itself: a thin rim faces sideways (sine of the tilt 0.9 at alpha 0), a dense core faces the camera. With f the flat sprite's normal (towards the camera) and L the direction to the sun, the lit colour is scaled by

k = (1 + 0.3 (n · L − f · L) + 0.15 (ny − fy)) × (1 − 0.12 · smoothstep(0.5, 1, α))

The side towards the sun gains what the far side loses, tops are lighter than bottoms (sky light from above), dense cores a little darker (light lost inside the puff). A puff seen face-on in its middle keeps its stock light. Only the lit part is scaled, the fog blend after it stays: out = c + (k − 1)(c − fog.a · fog.rgb).

White smoke is drawn thinner: at the game's opacity it comes out too white and too dense. Where the puff's own colour (its albedo, before light) has a luminance from 0.1 to 0.3, its opacity goes from the game's to 0.6 times it. Black exhaust keeps the game's opacity.

Softer edges

Where a sprite cuts through the ground it shows a hard line. The game fades sprites against the depth behind them; in the 110 shaders that do, the fade now runs over twice the distance, so puffs meet the ground softly.

Exhaust smoke: size and opacity

Card: Exhaust smoke. On at 150 % in Defaults, with a Strength stepper from 0 to 300 % in steps of 10.

Each vehicle uses shared particle definitions in initial.pak. This card scales the three components of Size/@Function and the alpha component of every Color/Key/@Value in exhaust_small.xml, exhaust_large.xml, exhaust_fog.xml and exhaust_heat_winter.xml under [media]\classes\particles. A strength P uses a factor P / 100. RGB values, colour multipliers, particle count and the heat distortion in exhaust_heat.xml stay as they are. Larger, more opaque puffs can cost frame rate where they overlap.

Every build starts from Next Gen's kept original initial.pak. At 100 %, the target attributes keep their original bytes; 0 % sets size and alpha to zero. The original remains available after a restart, so going back from 0 % works. Repeated Apply never multiplies a previously scaled value. Unticking the card removes this adjustment; Restore originals removes every module. Debris and splashes independently extends exhaust lifetime, and both edits are composed in the same archive build. Smoke glow and Smoke shape and softer edges still control lighting and shader opacity separately.

100 % refers to the kept original, including any earlier mod changes. No standalone Tuner state is imported. If that original already has invisible smoke, have the store verify the game's files and adopt those originals before tuning. The builder preserves the archive's entry names, order, compression methods and untouched payloads, verifies the new file before replacement, and rejects missing or unsupported exhaust definitions.

Sharper smoke and particles

Card: Sharper smoke and particles.

The game's 40 particle textures (the sfx_* files: smoke, dust, sparks, spray) are replaced with versions at twice their width and height, upscaled by the author with NVIDIA's DLSS 5 Visual Enhancer and stored in the game's own texture format (.pct, with its .pct_header). Each replaces its entry in boot.pak, stored as the game stores it. They remain Saber Interactive's assets and ship only for use with the game. Cost: four times the texture memory for these 40 textures.

Weather, and debris and splashes

Cards: Weather (nine rows), Debris and splashes. Both edit the game's own XML in initial.pak: what each daytime state carries, and how each particle type behaves.

What the game's states carry

The game cycles each region through its day states and their weather variants (day__1, day__1a, on newer maps up to day__1e). A state can carry rain or snow, <WeatherParticles Type MinIntensity MaxIntensity IntensityChangeInterval>, whose intensity is drawn anew within the range every interval, and moving cloud shadows, <CloudShadowMap Map Scale Speed Direction>. In the stock game, 16 regions have rain or snow in their variants, only the snow regions have weather at dusk and night, and only 39 of 199 states have cloud shadows.

Fireflies and pollen are weather types of the game's later engine (Expeditions); here they are two new files in classes\weather. Every attribute written is one the game's own files use.

Debris and splashes

Particle types are small XML files (one <CombineParticles> each). The card applies one rule per family:

  • Fragments (leaves, grass, twigs, dirt, stones thrown by the wheels): stock they vanish after 3 or 4 s. Now they live 30 to 40 s (LifeTime 30, Random 10) and collide with the ground, the truck and its wheels, with a 1 cm radius so a piece lies flat and a little bounce. Dandelion seeds float, so they only live longer (12 to 16 s).
  • Splashes (mud, water, snow, dirt bursts): a splash throws D times its drops (Subparticles Density, D = 4 by default, between 12 D and 64 D), the drops lie twice as long, the splash lasts twice as long and leaves twice the mud and water marks on the truck.
  • Puffs (exhaust, dust, steam) hang in the air half as long again (LifeTime × 1.5).
  • Snow splashes last three times as long, and their drops fall at half gravity with 0.6 of the air resistance and live 15 s: light snow hangs in the air.
  • Water spray: what a wheel throws spinning in water (water_splash_small, water_flow) and its drops (water_splash__subparticles, water_droplet__subparticles) fly further and are bigger. Stock the spray stops within half a metre: its air resistance is 3.0. The air resistance of the spray and its drops is now 0.4 of the game's (3.0 to 1.2, 1.0 to 0.4, 1.5 to 0.6), the spray's sprites are 1.5 times the size and live three times as long, the drops 1.5 times the scale. How hard the wheel throws stays the game's; with linear air resistance k a throw carries v / k, so the same throw goes about 2.5 times as far.

Cost: more particles alive at once, so more to draw; D is 4 by default.
