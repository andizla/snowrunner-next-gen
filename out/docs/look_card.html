Map days

Atmosphere · off by default

Four regions have no days of their own and show another region's. Maine shows two snow regions': snow in the air and winter light on a map without snow. This gives Maine Michigan's days, weather and sky; British Columbia, Austria and Washington have a row each.

What it does

Each map's level file names the day states it cycles through and its sky file. Three regions have none of their own and name another region's. Maine: its first map names Yukon's and its second Alaska's, two snow regions, so Maine gets their snowfall at dusk and at night and their light. This gives both maps Michigan's days, weather and sky. British Columbia: its maps name Michigan's, and the row gives them North Carolina's, which the game itself gives Washington, the region next to it. Austria: its first map names another region's days than its second, and the row gives the first the second's.

What you will notice

On Maine: no snow in the air, and the light, weather and sky of an autumn forest map.

What it costs

Nothing.

Options

A row for each region. Maine is on with the card. British Columbia, Austria and Washington are off: they are a matter of taste and have not been compared in the game. Each row has a third value, map by map, not looked at in the game yet. Maine: The Lowland has no snow on the ground and gets Michigan's wet days; Yellowrock National Forest has snow and ice and gets Yukon's spring with flurries instead of blizzards. Both are sets of Maine's own, so each map can be tuned on its own later. British Columbia: Duncan Bay, flooded, gets Wisconsin's summer with its heavy rain; North Peak National Park gets North Carolina's. Austria: Unterfeld, the farmland, gets Tennessee's summer; Foothills keeps the game's North Carolina. Washington is an autumn region whose three maps show North Carolina's summer: its row gives them Michigan's autumn, and map by map Selten River Mountains gets Zurdania's mountain autumn.

Good to know

Off by default until it has been looked at in the game. It changes a few names in your own level files (level_us_06_01.pak and the like), in place, and Restore writes the game's names back, after which the files are as they were, byte for byte. A level file that another mod changed is left as it is. A game update or the store's file check brings the game's names back: Apply again. Most values only name days the game ships. Maine map by map also adds twenty small files to initial.pak and names them in its load list. When a game update or the store's file check replaces initial.pak while Maine's level files still name them, Maine stops loading until Apply has run again.

Next Gen logo

Interface · on by default

What it does

Adds NEXT GEN under the game's logo wherever the interface shows it: on the title screen, the first loading screen, the main menu, the pause menu and the map.

What you will notice

The mod's name under the SnowRunner logo, so you can tell at a glance that it is installed.

What it costs

Nothing in the game. The first Apply keeps a copy of gfx.pak, several hundred MB, so that Restore can put it back.

Options

Start-up: the game's intro (its warning and the publisher logos, about 18 s), or the Next Gen splash only: the game goes straight to its first loading screen. Measured on 2026-10-17 with a warm disk cache: the title screen came 41 s after start with the intro and 24 to 25 s without. Off by default until it has been looked at.

Good to know

It changes gfx.pak, the file of the interface. The start-up row changes the game's start-up movie in the same file: two small edits to its script, and a game version whose movie differs keeps its intro (Apply says so). The title screen, the first loading screen and the main menu have a picture of their own for the logo, and those are replaced whole. On the pause menu and the map the logo shares a sheet with other pictures: there only the logo changes, so an interface mod's sheet keeps everything else, and a sheet with another layout is left as it is.
