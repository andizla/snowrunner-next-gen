Sun shadows: resolution, edges, softness, seams

Cards: SnowRunner Shadows (Resolution, Self-shadow fix), Shadow edges, Blocker search. Four changes to how the game's sun shadow is drawn and filtered.

Shadow mapping in one paragraph

Before the frame, the game draws the scene from the sun into a depth texture: each texel holds the distance from the sun to the nearest surface along that line. When a pixel of the frame is shaded, its position is projected into that texture; if the stored distance dmap is smaller than the pixel's own distance drecv, something lies between it and the sun:

lit = [ drecv − bias ≤ dmap(u, v) ]

One such test gives a hard edge with the texture's texels as stairs. Filters average many tests around the pixel (percentage-closer filtering, PCF), which softens the edge. SnowRunner keeps three cascades, one texture each for the near, middle and far range, side by side in one depth texture of 4608 × 1536 at its top setting (1536 × 1536 per cascade). A pixel uses the first cascade whose split distance lies beyond it: 15.5 m, 40.5 m, 100.5 m from the camera.

Resolution: a bigger shadow texture (SnowRunner Shadows, row Resolution)

No game file sets the shadow texture's size: it comes from the game's executable. The DLL recognises the texture when the game creates it (a depth texture three times as wide as high), creates it Factor times larger, and while it is the bound depth target scales every viewport and scissor rectangle the game sets by the same factor. The shaders read it with normalised coordinates (cascade offsets 0, 1/3, 2/3), so they need no change. Factor 3.5 is the largest that fits: 4608 × 3.5 = 16128, just under Direct3D 11's limit of 16384. A texel then covers 1/Factor² of the ground it did. Cost: at Factor 3.5 about 7 ms more per frame at 3840 × 2160. The card's default is 1x, because the rebuilt edges below give straight shadows at the game's own size.

Self-shadow fix (row Self-shadow fix)

To keep a surface from shadowing itself (shadow acne), the game draws the shadow texture with a slope-scaled depth bias: each primitive's depth is pushed back in proportion to its depth slope across one texel. A texture Factor times finer has texels Factor times smaller, so the same setting gives Factor times less bias, while the filters still reach as far as before. Surfaces at a grazing angle to the sun then start to shadow themselves, differently in each cascade (the dark side of a boulder flips at the 15 m border at dusk). With the fix on, the DLL swaps the game's rasterizer states, while the scaled texture is drawn, for copies with the slope-scaled bias multiplied by Factor. Every primitive gets exactly the bias it gets at the original size.

The game's filter, and why it shows stairs

The game's high quality shadow path (1269 material shaders: trucks, objects, terrain) runs three steps: pick the cascade, search for blockers to estimate how soft the edge should be (a penumbra size), then filter with 16 comparison taps at fixed Poisson-disc positions, stretched by the penumbra. Two things show:

  • At Factor 1 to 2 one texel covers 2 to 16 screen pixels near the camera, and the game's contact penumbra is about half a texel. Any filter narrow enough to keep contact shadows crisp shows the texel staircase.
  • When the penumbra spans more texels than 16 taps cover, every tap adds its own stair-stepped copy of the edge.

Shadow edges, the crisp choice: a tent over a grid

The first replacement filter (hq_grid) keeps the game's cascade choice, blocker search and penumbra, and replaces only the 16 taps. It takes a tent (a linear falloff, half width H = 1.6 kernel units, matching the spread of the stock taps) over a grid of bilinear comparison taps whose spacing is measured in real texels, with as many taps per axis as the width needs (2 to 8). A bilinear comparison tap already returns the lit fraction of four texels, weighted by position, so the grid integrates the tent over the texels:

lit = Σi,j wi wj · cmp(ui, vj),   wi = max(0, 1 − |xi| / H) / Σk max(0, 1 − |xk| / H)

No noise is used, so it needs no TAA to look clean.

Shadow edges, the default: revectorization

The default filter (hq_revec, written from Macedo and Apolinário, "Revectorization-Based Shadow Mapping", GI 2016, and Bondarev, "Shadow Map Silhouette Revectorization", I3D 2014) rebuilds the straight edge that the texels only sample.

  1. Four GatherCmp instructions read the depth tests of the 4 × 4 texels around the pixel. All equal: the pixel is plainly lit or shadowed, done (most pixels).
  2. The edge's main direction: more changes between rows than between columns means a mostly horizontal edge; the rest works in that frame. Then the change nearest the pixel in its own column, between rows R and R + 1.
  3. The edge is followed both ways, two columns per GatherCmp, up to 8 texels each way and inside this cascade. Where rows R and R + 1 stop differing, the texel beyond tells whether the edge stepped one row up or down (a step: the edge crosses the step's middle, so the step joins the fit and the follow goes on) or stopped (a corner, or a pattern that is neither). At most 4 steps each way join the fit.
  4. The edge as a line: the least-squares line through the steps' middles (xk, yk), each weighted by its distance from the pixel, wk = exp(−xk² / 2σ²) with σ = 4 texels:
    minimise Σk wk (yk − a − b xk)²   ⇒   b = (Σw Σwxy − Σwx Σwy) / (Σw Σwx² − (Σwx)²),   a = (Σwy − b Σwx) / Σw
    The line is used only if the middles lie within 0.3 texels RMS of it. A single step fades back to the run within the follow's reach (an L shape, placed as morphological antialiasing places it). When the first steps both ways go the same way (a U shape: a bump, the top of a round shape) the line bends in the middle of the pixel's run. With no step in reach, the edge lies flat, halfway between the rows.
  5. The lit fraction is the tent's profile across that line: a straight edge as soft as the game asked for, without the texel steps. For a signed distance s from the line, in texels, and tent half width H:
    lit(s) = ½ + s/H − s|s| / (2H²)   for |s| ≤ H, 0 or 1 beyond
    This is the integral of the normalised tent (1 − |x|/H)/H up to s.

Wide penumbrae, from a tent half width of 1.25 texels, take the tent over the texels: their steps are blurred already, and a 4 × 4 look cannot see an edge that far. From half that width the two are blended. Texture-like patterns (leaves, grass: many changes within the 4 × 4) take the tent as well.

Blocker search: how soft each edge is

Shadows are sharp where an object touches the ground and soften with distance. The game estimates this like PCSS: it averages the depths of the blockers found around the pixel, davg, and turns the ratio

p = (drecv − davg) / davg

into the penumbra size. The game's search reads 16 texels at fixed offsets; as they cross a blocker's edge one by one, the size jumps in visible steps. Next Gen's search (hq_blocker) reads 64 texels on a 4 × 4 grid of Gather fetches over the same area (the outer cells centred at 0.63 of the search radius, where the stock offsets reach), with the stock rule that every texel counts as min(depth, drecv − 0.001), and hands the same ratio back. The game turns it into the penumbra exactly as before; it only changes smoothly now.

The seam dither

At each split distance a shadow jumps from one cascade's texels and kernel to the next one's (texels 2 to 2.5 times larger, a kernel narrower by a factor e): a line across the ground 15.5 m from the camera. The seam patch replaces the cascade choice's compare. The pixel's depth z is raised by up to 8 % of itself with noise n per pixel and frame before the compare:

z' = z (1 + 0.08 n),   n ∈ [0, 1) (interleaved gradient noise, shifted by the game's time)

So over the last 0.08 / 1.08 = 7.4 % of each cascade's range (14.4 to 15.5 m, 37.5 to 40.5 m, 93.1 to 100.5 m) a growing share of pixels takes the next cascade, and the game's TAA averages the two. Pixels never take a nearer cascade.

Limits

  • Revectorization assumes straight edges. Detail smaller than a texel (thin branches, wires) cannot be rebuilt from texels that never saw it; there the filter keeps the tent.
  • The seam dither relies on TAA; with TAA off the band shows as a dotted transition.
  • Resolution costs time on the shadow pass and memory: Factor 2 is four times the texels.
